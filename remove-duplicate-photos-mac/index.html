How to remove duplicate photos on a Mac

Your Mac already has a free tool for exact duplicates. Here is how to use it, what it leaves behind, and how to clear the near-identical and blurry shots that take up most of the space.

Updated 23 October 2026 · Works on macOS Ventura (13) and later

1. Use the Duplicates album in Photos (free)

Since macOS Ventura, the Photos app finds duplicates by itself. It is the first thing to try, and it costs nothing.

  1. Open Photos.
  2. In the sidebar, under Utilities, click Duplicates. If you do not see it, Photos has not finished scanning your library yet, or it found no duplicates. Leave the Mac on and plugged in for a while, then look again.
  3. Check each set. When you are happy, click Merge next to the set, or select several sets and merge them together.

Merging keeps the highest quality version and combines the useful details of the others, such as captions, keywords and favourites. The extra copies go to Recently Deleted, where you can get them back for 30 days.

If you use iCloud Photos, merging and deleting happens on every device signed in to the same Apple Account. That is usually what you want, but it is worth knowing before you merge a few hundred sets.

2. What the Duplicates album misses

The Duplicates album looks for copies of the same picture: the same photo saved twice, or the same photo at a different size or file format. That leaves out most of what clutters a real library:

  • Near-identical takes. Ten shots of the same moment, taken a second apart, or a burst. They are different photos, so Photos keeps all of them.
  • Blurry photos. Out-of-focus or shaky shots that you would never choose to keep.
  • Photos outside the library. Pictures in folders in Finder, on an external drive, or straight from a camera card. The Duplicates album only looks inside Photos.

For those, you need a duplicate photo finder that compares how photos look, not only whether they are the same file.

3. Find near-identical and blurry shots with Lookalike

We make Lookalike for exactly the part the Duplicates album skips. It runs on your Mac, uses Apple's own image analysis, and uploads nothing.

  1. Open Lookalike and choose Photos Library or A Folder. macOS asks for permission the first time.
  2. Lookalike groups your photos into Duplicates, Similar shots and Blurry. In each set, the photo it would keep is the large one.
  3. Move the pointer over the kept photo to compare it in the loupe. Click a photo to keep or remove it, or double-click to keep only that one. The arrow keys move between sets.
  4. Press Remove. Library photos go to Recently Deleted in Photos, folder photos go to the Trash, so both can be restored.

Blurry photos and screenshots are shown to you but never selected for you. Nothing is removed until you confirm.

Before you delete anything

  • Back up first. A Time Machine backup takes the worry out of a big clean-up.
  • Empty Recently Deleted last. Removed photos stay there for 30 days. The space comes back only after you empty it.
  • Favourites first. If you mark the photos you love as favourites, Lookalike keeps them first in every set.
